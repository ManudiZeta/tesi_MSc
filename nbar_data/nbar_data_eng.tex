\part{Data - Monte Carlo comparison}
In this chapter, the comparison of the obtained $\bar{n}$ candidates cluster variables against those obtained from data is presented. The dataset is the latest available from Belle II data production, corresponding to an integrated luminosity of $358.27 fb^{-1}$. The same selections optimized for the $q\bar{q}$ Monte Carlo sample are applied, in both ISR and NO ISR cases. 

\subsection{Recoil mass distribution in data}
The recoil mass distribution, in both ISR and NO ISR cases, is shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:recM_data_pre}.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.49\textwidth]{nbar_DATA/images/recoil_mass_data_precutnog}
        \includegraphics[width=0.49\textwidth]{nbar_DATA/images/recoil_mass_data_precutisr}
    \caption{Recoil mass distribution in data. NO ISR case (left) and ISR case (right).}
    \label{fig:recM_data_pre}
\end{figure}

Unlike to what seen in the Monte Carlo simulation, in data no signal peak is observed at the anti-neutron mass. A completely different distribution is observed, with a prominent structure at low recoil mass values, suggesting that some additional contribution to the background is present, not included in the $q\bar{q}$ Monte Carlo cocktail. The structure has been associated to mis-identification of the particles in the recoil system $p \pi^-$.
$\\$
Hereby, the proton reconstruction, which has the stricter selection criteria, is investigated. Fig.~\ref{fig:proton_costheta_p} shows the 2D distribution of $cos(\theta)$ versus reconstructed momentum of proton, in data. It is possible to see several structures which were not present in the MC $q\bar{q}$ cocktail, and that cannot be associated to the signal $p \pi^- \bar{n} (\gamma_{ISR})$ channel.
\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.49\textwidth]{nbar_DATA/images/proton_costheta_p}
     \includegraphics[width=0.49\textwidth]{nbar_DATA/images/proton_costheta_p_isr}
    \caption{2D $\cos(\theta)$ vs momentum distribution in data. NO ISR case (left) and ISR case (right).}
    \label{fig:proton_costheta_p}
\end{figure}

Two structures are observed in the backward region ($cos(\theta) < -0.45$), one at proton momentum $p < 2$ GeV/c and one at $p \sim 3{-}4$ GeV/c. Furthermore, a structure is observed in the forward region ($cos(\theta) > 0.85$) across the full proton momentum range.
$\\$
Even if a strong selection on proton list has been performed, insufficient coverage of the PID detectors in the backward and forward regions prevents proper proton selection, allowing misidentification of the protons. Indeed, at such angular regions no TOP and no ARICH detectors contribute to the particle identification, and the PID is based mainly on $dE/dX$ on the CDC and SVD detectors. In particular, at high momenta, in practice, there is no separation power between different particle species. 
For example, background sources like the following one:

\begin{center}
$e^+ e^- \rightarrow e^+ e^-$, \hspace{2cm} $e^+ e^- \rightarrow \mu^+ \mu^-$,
\end{center}
where the positive track could be misidentified as the reconstructed proton in the backward-forward regions, while the $e^{\pm}$ or the $\mu^{\pm}$ could be easily misidentified as a pion since there are no strict selections for them.
$\\$
Therefore, a further selection can be performed on reconstructed proton $cos(\theta)$ variable, such as that $-0.45<cos(\theta)<0.85$, in order to exclude the structures observed in Fig.~\ref{fig:proton_costheta_p}. The following recoil mass distribution is shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:recM_nog_data_post}.
\begin{figure}[h]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.49\textwidth]{nbar_DATA/images/recoil_mass_data_postcutnog}
      \includegraphics[width=0.49\textwidth]{nbar_DATA/images/recoil_mass_data_postcutisr}
    \caption{Recoil mass distribution in data. NO ISR case (left) and ISR case (right). The selection $-0.45<cos(\theta)<0.85$ is applied on the reconstructed protons.}
    \label{fig:recM_nog_data_post}
\end{figure}
A structure at the anti-neutron mass is now observed, confirming that the discussed structures caused incorrect reconstruction of the recoil system. 
$\\$
The event selection criteria adopted for the remaining data analysis are as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $p$: $protonID > 0.9$, $dr<1$ cm, $|dz|<3$ cm and $-0.45<cos(\theta)<0.85$;
\item $\pi^-$: $pionID > 0.1$, $dr<1$ cm and $|dz|<3$ cm; 
\item $\bar{n}$: neutral clusters from ECL, number of hits $> 10$ and energy $<3$ GeV;
\item Recoil system: $0 \ GeV/c^2 <recoil \ mass < 2 \ GeV/c^2$ and in ECL acceptance;
\item $\alpha <0.10$ rad ($\sim 5.73^\circ)$;
\item No further charged particles in the Rest of Event (ROE).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Data/MC comparison}
The discussed $\bar{n}$ candidates cluster variables obtained in $q\bar{q}$ can be compared to those obtained from data. The resulting distributions, without sidebands subtraction, are shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:dataMC_nog_nosid} (NO ISR case) and in Fig.~\ref{fig:dataMC_isr_nosid} (ISR case). Those with sidebands subtraction are shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:dataMC_nog_sid} (NO ISR case) and in Fig.~\ref{fig:dataMC_isr_sid} (ISR case). The plots show also the pull distribution; for a histogram i-th bin, the pull is defined as:

\begin{center}
$pull_i = \frac{N_{i,data} - N_{i,MC}}{\sqrt{\sigma^2_{i,data} + \sigma^2_{i,MC}}}$,
\end{center}
where $N_{i,\mathrm{data}}$ and $N_{i,\mathrm{MC}}$ represent the number of entries in the $i$-th bin, and $\sigma^2_{i,\mathrm{data}}$ and $\sigma^2_{i,\mathrm{MC}}$ are the corresponding uncertainties. 
$\\$
$\\$
The Data/MC comparison plots reveal some discrepancies. While discrepancies in the inclusive distributions may arise from an incomplete description of all background processes in the Monte Carlo, after the sideband subtraction only the $\bar{n}$ contribution is expected to be studied, and even in this case not all distributions agree perfectly.
$\\$
The cluster Energy shows good Data/MC agreement (within $2\sigma$) in both ISR and no-ISR cases across the full energy range, with and without sidebands subtraction.
$\\$
The cluster E1E9 shows discrepancies ($>2\sigma$) with and without sidebands subtraction, suggesting that more spread shower shapes must be accounted for. This is confirmed by discrepancies in the Zernike moments, indicating different shower shapes in data and simulation.
$\\$
The cluster E9E21 shows small discrepancy, in particular for values close to 1, confirming what seen also in E1E9 distributions.
$\\$
Also cluster Lateral Momentum shows slight discrepancies in the shape, before sidebands subtraction but better agreement afterward (within $2\sigma$), without the structure at 0.3. It is notable that, while the inclusive plots show an excess at 0.3 in data compared to MC, due to a higher background contribution from $\gamma$, this structure disappears after the sideband subtraction, confirming the good quality of the background removal procedure.
$\\$
The cluster Second Moment distributions show good agreement between data and simulation, while the N Hits distributions do not. A better agreement is observed after the sideband subtraction, indicating that small‑sized (electromagnetic) clusters are effectively removed.
$\\$
In summary, comparing the distributions -even though the data sample has limited statistics- it is possible to conclude that, in general, the Monte Carlo reproduces the experimental distributions. While good agreement is observed for some variables, such as the deposited energy and the second moment, larger discrepancies are present for those more closely related to the shower shape, i.e., to the distribution of the energy deposited in the connected crystals of the clusters. Nevertheless, low statistic -particularly in the NO ISR case- suggests that additional measurements could target different channels, such as multi-pion final states  -$e^+e^- \to p\bar{n}\pi^-(\pi^+\pi^-)$ and so on (beyond the scope of this work).
$\\$
These discrepancies can be attributed to an imperfect description of the $\bar{n}$ interactions by the GEANT4 physics model.

\part{Conclusion}

In this thesis, the shape of the $\bar{n}$ hadronic showers has been studied, with particular focus on their simulation by the Belle II software. The analysis targets the channel $e^+ e^- \rightarrow p \pi^- \bar{n}$, considering both ISR and NO ISR cases.
$\\$
$\\$
The Monte Carlo signal sample study showed good agreement between the recoil kinematic variables and the generator-level $\bar{n}$ quantities, but the reconstructed $\bar{n}$ momentum proved unreliable for estimating the true $\bar{n}$ momentum. Furthermore, a 1C kinematic fit improved the recoil resolution.
$\\$
Energy leakages effects in $\bar{n}$ candidates -such as cluster split-off within the ECL and pre-showering before the ECL- have been studied. Secondary particles (mainly photons) are often associated to $\bar{n}$ clusters, creating small-sized clusters that distort cluster variable distributions. The distribution of the radial distance $\rho$ of all $\bar{n}$ candidates was studied, confirming that secondary particles are produced throughout the Belle II detector -particularly in the TOP sub-detector (pre-showering) and ECL sub-detector (cluster split-off).
$\\$
$\\$
The $q\bar{q}$ cocktail study showed the recoil mass distribution components from all cocktail channels. To reduce the background from channels not of interest, further cuts were applied, including those on the number of additional charged tracks and on the correlation angle.
$\\$
To remove the contribution from background channels still present in the signal region, the sidebands subtraction has been applied to the distributions to obtain the $\bar{n}$ candidate cluster variables from the $q\bar{q}$ cocktail. Before subtraction, several structures -such as those observed in E1E9, E9E21, cluster Lateral Momentum, and cluster Second Moment- were present not connected to the $\bar{n}$ response. These suggested small-sized electromagnetic showers, which were excluded after sidebands subtraction, leading to cluster variables close to those obtained in the signal sample analysis.
$\\$
$\\$
In conclusion, Data/MC comparison was assessed. Before sidebands subtraction, cluster variable comparisons showed discrepancies beyond $2\sigma$ uncertainties between data and Monte Carlo. After subtraction, improved agreement was observed, though low statistics suggest further studies with more channels containing $\bar{n}$ in the final state. These can include $e^+e^- \to p\bar{n}\pi^-(\pi^+\pi^-)$ or channels also involving charged kaons. They may be affected by a less precise recoil mass distribution due to the larger number of charged particles used in the reconstruction, but they could increase the total statistics and allow for more detailed differential studies. The global results agree with other Belle II studies on neutral particle hadronic interactions \cite{SavinoLongo2026}, suggesting that further investigation of GEANT4 Physics lists for anti-neutron hadronic interactions is needed. Indeed, the GEANT4 software provides several physics lists that implement different models for hadronic interactions, and in this thesis the standard list used by the Belle II software has been adopted. A better agreement could potentially be achieved by using alternative physics lists.
$\\$
In any case, considering the obtained results, analyses based on the $\bar{n}$ shower shapes are affected by systematic uncertainties that could be reduced with improved Data/MC agreement.
